# Platform ADR-0005: The service-to-service feed credential

- **Status:** Proposed
- **Date:** 2026-09-14
- **Deciders:** Sodiq (platform architect); option (a) chosen 2026-09-14
- **Satisfies:** auth ADR-0019 condition 2 ("the machine-to-machine credential ADR lands
  before the feed is built") and closes its blocking risk W1
- **Consumed by:** every publisher of a `GET /events` feed (auth, xal-org, xal-onboarding,
  xal-notify) and every consumer of one

> **Provenance.** Drafted as the xal-org spec's companion document
> (`adr-draft-0005-service-to-service-feed-credential.md`, 2026-09-14) and landed here
> verbatim. Its seven numbered decision points were presented ahead of the body for
> approval and are moved into the Decision section here, unchanged; `Alternatives
> considered` is nested under Decision to match ADR-0002.

## Context

Auth ADR-0019 chose a transactional outbox served as a cursor-paged HTTP pull feed, and
recorded as its first blocking risk that "auth has no machine-to-machine credential; the feed
cannot be authenticated without one — its own ADR, must land first." It framed the question
with bounded-context weight: does auth own service identities, and where would a new
credential type sit in the ADR-0003 flag table?

Three things have changed since it was written:

- **There is a second publisher.** xal-org (spec v1.0.0) emits its own feed, and
  xal-onboarding and xal-notify will consume feeds from B2. The credential is therefore an
  estate-wide contract, not an auth feature, and ADR-0019's own revisit trigger 4 ("a second
  publisher appears") has fired.
- **Deployments are per client.** Clients deploy a selected set of units behind a gateway
  (offering shape, 2026-09-14), and the composition engine "will eventually generate per
  composed solution" the manifest, reconcile script and Terraform modules
  (deployment-conventions). Secrets are already set by the reconcile script from CI env and
  live only in the platform store. A credential generated at composition time fits that model
  with nothing new.
- **Async-first (ADR-0004) defines the feed poll as the event dependency itself**, a read one
  hop deep, and requires every sync edge to be declared with timeout and fallback. The
  credential is what makes a declared edge the only edge.

The counter-argument on record (xal-company PR #5): a managed broker supplies its own
credential, removing W1 outright, and moves the read surface off the login-critical-path
service, satisfying ADR-0019 condition 6 by construction. That argument is weighed below and
is not dismissed; it is the path this decision is designed to be compatible with.

Scale, stated honestly: at B3 the estate has three feed edges (onboarding → org,
org → onboarding, notify → onboarding) in one deployment. This decision plans for tens of
edges per deployment, not thousands.

## Decision

1. **Every event feed (`GET /events`) is protected by a per-deployment, per-edge feed credential.** One credential per (consumer unit, publisher unit) pair in a client deployment. It is a random secret of at least 256 bits of entropy, generated by the composition engine when it produces a composed solution's artifacts; until the composition engine exists, the same generation runs as a step in the publisher's idempotent reconcile-first provisioning script (deployment-conventions Pattern A).
2. **Distribution is one-way and hashed on the publisher side.** The consumer receives the plaintext secret as a platform-store secret (`XAL_FEED_CREDENTIAL__<publisher>`). The publisher receives an allow-list of `(consumer name, SHA-256 of the secret)` pairs as a platform-store secret. Nothing is written to a manifest, Terraform, its state, or a repo. The publisher compares in constant time and resolves the hash to the consumer name; that name is what per-credential rate limiting (ADR-0019 R1) and the metrics `feed_distinct_clients` and `feed_consumer_lag_seq` key on, so the consumer registry stays observed, not declared, exactly as ADR-0019 requires.
3. **Scope is the feed, nothing else.** The credential is presented as `Authorization: Bearer <secret>` on `GET /events` only. It is rejected on every other route, and access tokens from xal-auth are rejected on `GET /events`. The feed is not routed by the deployment's gateway; consumers reach publishers over the deployment's private network, and the credential travels only over TLS.
4. **The allow-list is derived from the registry.** A publisher's allow-list is exactly the set of consumers whose registry entries declare an event dependency on it. An undeclared consumer has no credential and cannot poll. This makes §9's registry-honesty rule enforceable at the credential level for feed edges.
5. **Rotation is a reconcile step, gated by a human.** The publisher accepts a set (current and previous hash per consumer) so rotation is two reconcile passes with no downtime. Rotation on suspicion is immediate and human-triggered (P6, granting access); scheduled rotation is deferred (below).
6. **xal-auth is not changed.** No client-credentials grant, no service account, no new row in the ADR-0003 flag table. ADR-0019 W1's question, whether auth owns service identities, is answered "not now": service identity is a composition concern, and the only thing auth builds is the publisher side of this decision on its own feed, which ADR-0019's estimate already contains.
7. **This is not a general service identity.** If a unit ever needs an authenticated call to another unit outside a feed poll (a sync read on a person-facing route, or a sync write), that is revisit trigger 1 below and a new decision, not an extension of this one.

### Alternatives considered

**(b) A client-credentials grant issued by xal-auth (RFC 6749 §4.4).** Each unit holds a
client id and secret, obtains a short-lived JWT with `sub` = the unit, and publishers verify
it offline against auth's JWKS. Genuine advantages: one identity system, offline verification,
rotation by key rotation, and an audit trail in auth. Rejected now, for four reasons that
ADR-0019 W1 anticipated. It is a new capability in auth, outside ADR-0019's estimate, that
must be placed in the ADR-0003 flag table. It makes auth a runtime dependency of every feed
poll (token acquisition and refresh in every consumer, in every language), where ADR-0019's
stated aim was that token validation "survives auth being down". It gives auth ownership of
service identities, a bounded-context decision this estate has not needed to make. And it
couples the composition engine to auth's client registry, so a composed solution cannot be
generated without a live auth to register clients in. Deferred with a trigger (below), not
closed.

**(c) A managed broker's own credential (PR #5).** Under Synadia Cloud or another managed
broker, each deployment gets a broker account and per-client credentials, publishers stop
serving a read surface, and W1 disappears. Rejected here only because it reopens ADR-0019,
which the hold ruling and xal-org spec §6.6 decline to do at two publishers. Noted as
forward-compatible: under (c) the credential problem becomes "a secret generated at
composition time and injected per unit", which is the same shape as (a). Choosing (a) now
does not prejudice (c) later; the consumer-side polling code is what changes, not the secret
distribution.

**(d) Mutual TLS between units.** Strongest binding of identity to transport. Rejected for
now because per-deployment certificate provisioning and rotation is infrastructure a solo
operator would run, and Fly's private network already provides transport isolation. Deferred
(trigger: a compliance requirement), consistent with review 0.1's mTLS deferral.

**(e) No authentication on the private network.** Rejected. ADR-0019 R1 requires
per-credential rate limiting, which needs an identity; registry honesty for feed edges needs
one too; and a private network is a boundary, not an authorization.

**(f) A single shared secret per deployment.** Simpler to distribute, rejected because it
cannot key per-consumer rate limits or per-consumer lag metrics, and a leak grants every feed
to every holder.

## Consequences

**Positive**

- No new infrastructure and no change to auth's bounded context; W1 closes.
- ADR-0019 R1 (per-credential rate limiting) and its metric-based revisit triggers
  (`feed_distinct_clients`, `feed_consumer_lag_seq`) get their identity for free.
- Registry honesty becomes enforceable for feed edges: no declaration, no credential.
- Forward-compatible with a broker (c): the same composition-time secret shape.
- Secrets follow deployment-conventions unchanged: platform store only, set by the reconcile
  script, never in manifest, state or repo; hashed at rest on the publisher side.

**Negative / cost**

- **Edges multiply secrets.** N consumers × M publishers per deployment, each a rotation
  item; three at B3, and the composition engine must own the generation to keep this
  mechanical.
- **The composition engine does not exist yet.** The interim is a step in each publisher's
  provisioning script. That is a duplication the engine must absorb, and it is on B2's
  critical path because xal-onboarding cannot be tested end to end without credentials on
  both feeds.
- **Rotation is two reconcile passes**, and rotation on suspicion is human-triggered; there
  is no automatic scheduled rotation until the trigger below.
- **A leaked secret grants one feed in one deployment** until rotated. Blast radius is small
  by construction but not zero; detection relies on the per-credential metrics.
- **Consumers still hand-write polling**, unchanged from ADR-0019; this decision adds one
  header to that code.

**Deferred, with triggers**

- **Client-credentials grant in auth (b).** Trigger: a unit needs an authenticated call to
  another unit that is not a feed poll (a sync read on a person-facing route, or any sync
  write), or a third party outside the deployment needs to poll a feed.
- **Scheduled rotation.** Trigger: first real client data in a deployment; until then,
  rotate on suspicion.
- **mTLS (d).** Trigger: a compliance requirement.

## Revisit trigger

Reopen when any of these becomes true:

1. ADR-0004's trigger 1 or 2 fires (a two-hop sync edge, or a sync write): a real service
   identity is needed and (b) returns.
2. ADR-0019's trigger 3 fires (`feed_distinct_clients` reaches 3 on any feed) or a broker is
   adopted: move to the broker's credential model (c).
3. A consumer outside the deployment (a client's own system) asks to poll a feed: this
   credential is not for third parties.
4. Edges per deployment exceed what one reconcile pass can rotate without downtime.

## Confidence and open risks

**Confidence: medium.** High confidence that a composition-time secret is sufficient for feed
reads at this scale and that it changes nothing in auth. Lower confidence in the interim
distribution path, which lives in provisioning scripts until the composition engine exists;
that is the part most likely to be done twice.

Open risks are listed in the frontmatter.
